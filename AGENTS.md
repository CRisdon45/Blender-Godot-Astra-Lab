# Courtyard look-development direction

Cody's approved direction is a clean anime / architectural-illustration world,
with **water as the visual star, under a separate and higher quality bar**.

- Research primary artist tutorials and inspect references before major changes
  to a material family. Record what was read, what is adapted, and licensing in
  `docs/STYLE_DIRECTION_AND_WATER.md`. Do not claim to have watched an entire video
  when only its introduction or write-up was accessible.
- Adapt geometry, grouped normals, material marks, and light relationships to the
  real-time Godot scene. Do not apply one flat toon filter to every surface.
- Keep full plant volume, clear silhouettes, broad shadow groups and restrained
  ink. Avoid tiny foliage noise, repeated fine stripes and universal heavy grain.
- Water must preserve depth/shelf/step readability, convincing reflection and
  refraction, receiver-space caustics and geometry-coupled feature interaction.
  Judge it at low and high angles, moving cameras, flow-on/off and day/night.
  A beautiful isolated still is not final water acceptance.
- Keep studies opt-in until visually reviewed. Preserve original authored assets,
  scene entry points and baseline images; no merge to main without approval.
- Use actual Godot frames as evidence, not generated illustration mockups.
  Python source tests, complete PNGs and software-renderer timings are not artistic
  acceptance or Android GPU performance evidence. Retain known import errors.
- Prefer direct repository work and bounded Linux review jobs. Do not delegate
  routine work to paid coding agents. No runtime AI dependencies in the product.

Current water study: `godot/courtyard_hero_water.tscn` (F6). Its trial optics and
lighting controls are separate from the approved foliage scene. See the research
ledger and water notes before changing their assumptions.
